**Date:1 June 2026
**Post:47.5% of all scrolling on AIO SERPs goes backward, compared to 27% without an AIO on the page.

Among users who reverse direction on an AIO SERP, the median user spends nearly half their total scroll going back up the page.

Scrolling stopped being a one-way trip. On an AIO SERP, the user reads, weighs, scrolls past, then circles back to re-read.

The implication for meta descriptions: your listing is no longer getting one impression. It's getting 2, sometimes 3. The first impression earns the read on the way down. The second impression earns the click on the way back up.

The tactical implication: think about how to make your meta title and description scroll-stopping without sacrificing trust.

## POST 2

**Date:May 2026
**Post:The same Google user behaves differently in AI Overviews and AI Mode.

I broke down a new clickstream study of 846k US Google sessions from Feb and March 2026. Prior public Google SERP mouse-tracking studies measured dozens of people. This one analyzed tens of thousands.

The pattern: AI Mode is autoplay. AI Overviews is the Netflix browse.

In AI Mode, users accept what they're handed. 88% take the AI's shortlist as-is, 74% pick the top result, and 64% click nothing at all. Closed loop.

In AI Overviews, the same user starts comparing. Cursor coverage hits 83% of the visible page (vs. 66% without an AIO). Stillness rises from 29% to 44%. Back-scrolling makes up nearly half of all scroll activity. The user reads, weighs, scrolls past, then circles back.

AI Mode optimization is a visibility problem at the model layer. AI Overview optimization is a comparison problem on the SERP itself.

## POST 3

**Date:May 2026
**Post:The citation gap between minimal and high reasoning isn't constant. It scales with funnel stage.

I ran 5 buyer journey stages (Problem, Exploration, Comparison, Validation, Selection) through GPT-5.2 twice, once with minimal reasoning, once with high. Data via Semrush's AI Visibility Toolkit.

3 patterns held across 20 journeys:
1. Citation rate gap is +35pp at the Problem stage and only +5pp at Validation. High reasoning treats early-funnel prompts as research tasks. Minimal mode answers from memory.
2. Fan-out queries peak at Comparison: 24 sub-queries per response under high reasoning vs 5.5 under minimal. Selection runs 15.4 vs 2.6.
3. Selection has the widest per-response variance: 0 to 40 fan-out queries on the same cohort. Bounded prompts (RFP drafts, 0% APR vs bank loan) run zero. Open-ended product builds (a $3,000 home gym shopping list) run 28 to 40.

A fan-out under high reasoning for a CRM comparison breaks into separate retrievals for API rate limits per vendor, SOC 2 compliance, SSO support, webhook architecture, OAuth, pricing tiers, and change-data-capture support. Each becomes its own search.

The brand that wins isn't the one ranking for the parent prompt. It's the one whose docs surface clean for each sub-query.
